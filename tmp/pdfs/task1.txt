9.1.4 INTRODUCTION

     Company Background & Problem Statement

Founded in 2025, Verichat is a prominent organization operating within the
specialized medical logistics and distribution sector. The company is dedicated to
supplying healthcare facilities with critical resources, ranging from bulk personal
protective equipment (PPE) to specialized medical hardware. As the organization has
grown to meet regional healthcare demands, the complexity of its client
relationships and supply chain has expanded proportionately.

Despite this growth, the company's administrative infrastructure has not kept pace.
Currently, the organization relies on a highly fragmented, legacy approach to manage
its operations. Interaction records, purchasing preferences, and deal statuses are
scattered across disjointed systems, disconnected spreadsheets, and manual logs.
This lack of centralization creates a critical "visibility gap" across the organization.
Management struggles to accurately forecast revenue, while sales and administrative
staff waste valuable time cross-referencing multiple sources to generate a standard
quote. Ultimately, these disjointed manual processes introduce significant
operational friction, hindering overall efficiency and slowing down the sales cycle.

     Document Purpose & System Introduction

The Proposed Solution: Verichat CRM system. To resolve these systemic bottlenecks,
we at Nova (tema) propose developing and implementing Verichat, a modern,
specialized enterprise management and customer relationship management (CRM)
system. The Verichat CRM system is engineered specifically to eliminate current
operational friction points by centralizing disjointed data into actionable business
intelligence.

Verichat CRM system addresses the organization's visibility gap by shifting the
workflow to a client-focused, single-screen view. Through the implementation of
Unified Client Profiles, the system consolidates historical interaction records, key
personnel directories, and purchasing preferences into one accessible directory. This
ensures that every interaction--from initial contact to long-term partnership--is
accurately tracked.

Furthermore, the system delivers immediate business value by replacing manual
administrative tasks with automated workflows. Key features include automated
quotation tools that generate itemized, professional PDFs and dynamic executive
dashboards that provide real-time revenue forecasting.
Above all, Verichat CRM is designed with a core mandate of "User-Friendly Simplicity."
It ensures that sales and administrative staff can operate at peak efficiency and
leverage advanced data tools without requiring a technical background. The resulting
architecture is both accessible for daily users and scalable enough to support
Verichat's future expansion into broader regional medical distribution.

9.1.7 User Stories

System Administrator
 As a System Administrator, I want to configure role-based permissions for each
user type so that Sales Reps, Support Engineers, and clients only see the data
relevant to their role.
 As a System Administrator, I want a global analytics dashboard showing usage,
error rates, and system load so that I can spot problems before they affect users.
 As a System Administrator, I want to update Firestore Security Rules from a
controlled deployment process so that access-control changes are tested before
they reach production.

Sales Representative / Account Executive

 As a Sales Rep, I want to view dynamic lead scores for incoming inquiries so that I
can prioritise urgent, high-value medical deals.

 As a Sales Rep, I want to move deals through pipeline stages on a visual board so
that I always know the status of every account I manage.

 As a Sales Rep, I want to generate a branded quotation PDF directly from a deal
so that I can send pricing to a client without leaving the platform.

Procurement Manager (Client)

 As a Corporate Procurement Manager, I want to place single-click reorders for
medical consumables so that facility stockouts are avoided.

 As a Corporate Procurement Manager, I want to log a complaint with photo
evidence and receive a ticket number so that I have proof of the issue and can track
its resolution.

 As a Corporate Procurement Manager, I want to see the delivery status of my last
three orders so that I can plan around delays.

Field Support Engineer / Technical Support

 As a Support Engineer, I want issues automatically routed by SLA parameters so
that critical equipment maintenance deadlines are met.

 As a Support Engineer, I want to log service history against a specific piece of
equipment so that repeat faults are visible to the next engineer who picks up the
ticket.

 As a Support Engineer, I want a mobile view of my assigned tickets so that I can
update job status from a client site without a laptop.

Medical Student

 As a Medical Student, I want a Quick-Cart interface so that I can buy stethoscopes
and lab coats without administrative delay.

 As a Medical Student, I want to see student-discounted pricing automatically
applied at checkout so that I don't need to apply a code manually.

 As a Medical Student, I want order confirmation and tracking on my phone so that
I know when training equipment will arrive before a practical session.

Storage Technology Rationale & Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD / JSON Schema)

Storage Technology Rationale

Verichat's persistence layer is built on Cloud Firestore, the NoSQL, document-oriented database
that already sits at the base of the layered architecture described in Section 6.2, directly beneath
the Hosting, Auth, and Functions layers shown in the topology diagram in Section 6.3. Firestore
stores data as documents grouped into collections rather than as rows in tables and, unlike a
relational engine, does not require every document in a collection to share identical fields
(Firebase, 2025). Four factors specific to Verichat's requirements drove this choice over a
relational alternative such as PostgreSQL.

           Catalogue flexibility: The specialised medical catalogue described spans very different
           product families: PPE, laboratory equipment, hospital infrastructure, home care and infant
           care, each carrying its own attributes. A relational schema would force a wide, mostly-
           empty products table or a costly set of subtype joins; Firestore's document-centric model
           lets each product document carry only the fields its own category needs (FasterCapital,
           2025).

           Real-time pipeline updates: Journey 2 requires the Sales Progress Tracker board to reflect
           a deal's stage the moment it changes. Firestore's client SDKs support live snapshot
           listeners that push updates to every connected screen without the Nova Team having to
           build or run a separate WebSocket layer, the same mechanism that underlies the
           Observer-pattern stock warning already described in Section 6.1.

           Native stack integration & security: Hosting, Auth, and Functions are already Firebase
           products; Firestore avoids a separate database driver or connection pool. More
           importantly, the existing Role-Based Access Control boundaries are enforced by Firestore
           Security Rules at the data layer itself; rules run server-side on every request and, as
           already noted in Section 6.3, cannot be bypassed by modifying client code.

           A usage-based cost model: Firestore's pay-per-operation pricing is what produces the
           tiered figures in Section 9.1, R0 at 100 users scaling to roughly R2 374.84/month at 10,000
           users, because Verichat is billed for the reads, writes, and storage it actually consumes,
           rather than for a fixed-size instance sized for peak load in advance.

           The trade-off is that Firestore has no server-side JOIN operator, so a single query cannot
           combine data from two collections (Estuary, 2026). Verichat mitigates this in two ways:
           first, by deviating a small number of frequently read fields (e.g., copying a client's name
           onto its deal documents so the pipeline board can render a card without a second read);
           second, by resolving cross-collection references within Cloud Functions rather than in the
           client. This second point is precisely the role already assigned to the One-Click Quotation
           Generator's Facade pattern in Section 6.1, which pulls the client profile, deal, and product
           catalog together on the server before returning a single PDF.

     Architecture Patterns
Verichat will be using a layered (N-tier) architecture: presentation, business logic/API, and
data are separated so each layer only calls into the layer below it, and each can be
developed, scaled, and tested independently.
     Cloud

Firebase Security Rules run server-side on every read and write request before it reaches
the database, and unlike client-side validation, they cannot be bypassed by modifying the
app, since they're evaluated on Firebase's servers rather than in the client. That is the
boundary that keeps a client from ever reading another client's records directly,
regardless of what the front-end code does. On top of that, Google Cloud's own network-
security guidance for segmentation (isolate workloads, centralize firewall policy, and
prevent cross-boundary data movement) is applied at the project level: Verichat will use
separate Firebase projects for development, staging, and production, each with its own
IAM roles and Security Rules, so a compromised or misconfigured dev environment can't
reach production data.

All client-to-service traffic runs over HTTPS/TLS the browser and mobile app load the
static bundle from Firebase Hosting over HTTPS, authenticate through Firebase Auth over
HTTPS, and read/write Firestore through the client SDK, which communicates over an
encrypted gRPC channel. Privileged operations (e.g. generating a quotation PDF) go
through Cloud Functions instead of direct client writes, and Cloud Functions use the
Admin SDK to reach Firestore with elevated, server-only trust.
9.1.13 SECURITY

Network Security and Data in Transit
Securing the communication channels between the client interfaces and the backend
infrastructure is critical to prevent interception, data tampering, and man-in-the-middle
(MitM) attacks.
Transport Layer Security (TLS 1.3): All external and internal network traffic is securely
routed using the TLS 1.3 protocol. The implementation of TLS 1.3 provides forward
secrecy. This ensures that even if a server's cryptographic keys are compromised in the
future, the contents of previous communications remain entirely protected and encrypted
(William Newhouse, 2017).

Mitigating Common Vulnerabilities (OWASP Guidelines)
To defend against prevalent exploitation techniques, the application's security model
actively addresses risks outlined in the Open Worldwide Application Security Project
(OWASP) Top 10, a globally recognized standard representing the most critical security
risks to web applications.

Broken Access Control: OWASP identifies broken access control as a primary threat that
allows attackers to function as standard users or administrators to gain unauthorized
access. To mitigate this, the system uses strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) to
create definitive trust boundaries for data access, ensuring users cannot view or modify
cross-account records.

Cryptographic Failures: Cryptographic failures occur when stored or transmitted data is
compromised due to inadequate encryption strength or broken algorithms. To prevent
this, all sensitive information is encrypted at rest, and the use of weak or hardcoded
cryptographic keys within the source code is strictly prohibited to defend against identity
theft.

Potential Threats and System Resilience
To establish a holistic defense strategy against sophisticated threats like unauthorized
intrusions and automated data scraping, the application lifecycle aligns closely with the
core functions of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF).
Identify & Protect: The architecture identifies critical assets and implements strict
safeguards--such as encryption and access controls--designed to limit and contain the
impact of potential cybersecurity events.

Detect & Respond: The system relies on continuous monitoring tools and logging
mechanisms to identify unusual network activities, enabling a swift response protocol to
contain incidents before they escalate.

Recover: To minimize operational impact in the event of a successful cyber attack,
structured database backups and restoration protocols ensure that impaired services can
facilitate a quick return to normal operations.

Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) Prevention and Mitigation

Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) occurs when an application includes untrusted data in a web
page without proper validation or escaping, allowing attackers to execute malicious scripts
in the victim's browser (OWASP Cheat Sheet Series). . To ensure the application remains
resilient against both Reflected and Stored XSS attacks, a defense-in-depth approach is
applied using the following techniques:

Context-Aware Output Encoding: The primary defense mechanism against XSS is ensuring
that all user-supplied data returned to the browser is strictly treated as display content
rather than executable code (OWASP Cheat Sheet Series).

Input Validation and Sanitization: All incoming data is subjected to strict allow-list
validation on the server side. Data that requires rich text formatting is processed through
a dedicated sanitization library to strip out hazardous HTML tags and JavaScript event
handlers before it is committed to the database.

Content Security Policy (CSP): As an additional layer of security, a robust Content
Security Policy (CSP) is implemented via HTTP response headers. The CSP restricts the
sources from which executable scripts can be loaded and completely disables the
execution of inline scripts (unsafe-inline), significantly reducing the attack surface even if
an XSS vulnerability is inadvertently introduced.

Application of Core Security Principles and Additional Risk Mitigation

Beyond XSS and network defenses, the application's architecture is grounded in
foundational security principles designed to mitigate other critical vectors, ensuring the
Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA Triad) of the system.

Version Control, Branching & Gitflow Strategy

Nova manages the Verichat codebase on GitHub using the Gitflow branching model, first published
by Vincent Driessen and formalised as one of Atlassian's standard workflow strategies (Atlassian,
2025). Gitflow assigns a specific purpose to each branch and defines exactly when branches
interact (Atlassian, 2025), which suits a three-person student team that needs an explicit, low-risk
path from a feature under development through to a production release, rather than committing
directly against one shared branch. Gitflow is widely acknowledged to add more overhead than a
simpler trunk-based approach and has fallen out of favour for teams shipping continuously
(Atlassian, n.d); for Verichat, this trade-off is acceptable, because the team releases in scheduled
batches against the three fixed Firebase environments already defined in Section 6.3, and the
extra structure gives less experienced contributors a clear checkpoint before any change reaches
real client data.

The Gitflow Branching Model is a structured Git workflow introduced by Vincent Driessen,
designed for projects with well-defined release cycles. It uses multiple branch types with specific
purposes, ensuring clear separation between development. releases, and urgent fixes (Atlassian,
2025).
To keep the history traceable, branches are named feature/<issue-number>-<short-description>
(e.g. feature/23-quick-cart-checkout), referencing the same GitHub Issues already used to log bugs
during Hypercare in Section 10, and commits follow the Conventional Commits format (feat:, fix:,
docs:, chore:) so that changes map back to a user story from Section 2.3 (Atlassian, 2025). Both
main and develop are protected branches: direct pushes are disabled, at least one of the other two
team members must approve a pull request, and the automated checks described in Section 8.2
must pass before GitHub allows the merge button to be used.

9.1.16 CHANGE MANAGEMENT

This section addresses the strategic approach to ensuring the successful adoption of the Verichat
CRM and Medical Logistics system by both the organization (Verichat management and internal staff)
and its external client base. Effective change management is as critical as technical execution; a
well-built system that is not adopted delivers no value. This plan follows a structured framework
adapted from Kotter's 8-Step Change Model and aligned with Verichat's operational context.

     How and why will the organization adopt your software?

Why the Organization Adopts It: The primary risk to any new system implementation is not
technical failure but human resistance to change. Verichat's sales and administrative staff are
accustomed to existing manual processes. The adoption strategy below is designed to minimize
disruption, build confidence in the system, and achieve full operational adoption within 8 weeks of
go-live.
How the Organization Adopts It: VeriChat management will be engaged as active champions of the
new system. The following steps address organizational-level adoption:

           � Executive Sponsorship: Mrs. J. Maredza (client/management) will be designated as the
           internal champion for the VeriChat system rollout. Her visible endorsement of the
           platform to staff is critical to driving adoption.

           � Business Case Communication: Management will communicate to all staff the specific
           pain points the system resolves -- fragmented records, slow quotations, manual complaint
           tracking, framing the system as a tool that reduces their workload rather than adding
           complexity.

           � Policy Alignment: Management will update internal standard operating procedures (SOPs)
           to formalize use of the VeriChat system for all client interactions, inquiry management,
           and complaint resolution. This transitions system use from optional to standard practice.

           � Progress Review Meetings: Bi-weekly review meetings with management during the first 8
           weeks post-launch will track system adoption rates, identify friction points, and allow
           rapid course correction.
     How and why will the users adopt your software?

Why the Users Adopt It: Users adopt the system because it reduces manual workloads, minimizes
administrative friction, and provides an intuitive, accessible interface tailored to their specific
roles.
How the Users Adopt It: End-user adoption is addressed through a structured, role-specific
onboarding programme:

           � Role-Based Training: Each user role (Sales Representative, Procurement Manager, System
           Administrator) will receive a tailored training session focused specifically on the screens
           and features relevant to their daily tasks. No user will be trained on the full system -- only
           what they need.

           � Hands-On Walkthrough: Training will include live, guided walkthroughs of the system
           using real-world scenarios (e.g., 'Generate a quotation for a hospital client' or 'Log a
           complaint for a broken ventilator').

           � User Guide Distribution: A comprehensive User Guide (see Task 3 deliverable) will be
           provided digitally to all users. The guide includes annotated screenshots, step-by-step
           instructions, and a FAQ section for common user errors.

           � Buddy System: During the first two weeks post-launch, experienced users (early adopters
           or team members who participated in UAT) will be paired with resistant or less confident
           users to provide peer support.

           � Feedback Mechanism: A simple in-system feedback form will allow users to flag confusing
           screens or missing features during the adoption period, feeding directly into the change
           request process.
